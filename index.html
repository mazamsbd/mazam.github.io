Research


My research focuses on the development of advanced polymer electrolyte membranes (PEMs), which are critical components of fuel cells and water electrolysis systems. My work aims to address key challenges in the hydrogen energy sector by developing high-performance, durable, and cost-effective membrane materials.

My expertise lies in the design, synthesis, and functionalization of novel polymeric materials. I specialize in tailoring the morphology and microstructure of polymer membranes to optimize their ionic conductivity, chemical stability, and mechanical strength, which are essential for achieving efficient and durable electrochemical performance. My research also involves advanced characterization techniques to investigate the physicochemical, structural, and morphological properties of these materials at both the molecular and macroscopic levels.

Prior to my postdoctoral appointment, I earned my Ph.D. in Polymer Chemistry from Incheon National University, where I established a strong foundation in polymer science and advanced functional materials. Throughout my academic career, I have maintained a strong commitment to advancing sustainable and green energy technologies, particularly hydrogen-based energy systems. I have published my research in reputable scientific journals and actively participated in international conferences, fostering academic collaboration and contributing to the advancement of clean energy research.

With a strong passion for sustainable energy solutions and a robust technical background in polymeric materials and electrochemical technologies, I aspire to contribute to the global transition toward a low-carbon energy future by developing innovative membrane materials that can enhance the efficiency, durability, and scalability of hydrogen technologies. ome papers are highlighted below.

Papers
Development of High-Performance Polymer Electrolyte Membranes through the Application of Quantum Dot Coatings to Nafion Membranes
Kyungwhan Min(╪), Abu Zafar Al Munsur(╪), Sae Yane Paek, Soomin Jeon, So Young Lee, Tae-Hyun Kim,
ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, 2023, 15, (12):15616-15624. DOI:10.1021/acsami.3c01289

This paper investigates the use of N-doped carbon quantum dots (CQDs) as an additive, which were synthesized from citric acid and urea and subsequently applied to Nafion-212 membranes via spin coating to ensure the formation of thin and uniform coating layers. The resulting CQD-coated Nafion membranes exhibited reduced hydrogen crossover, high ionic conductivity, and excellent single-cell performance. In addition, the study systematically evaluated the effects of CQD incorporation on the ionic conductivity, mechanical properties, hydrogen permeability, electrochemical properties, and single-cell performance of the fabricated Nafion membranes.
